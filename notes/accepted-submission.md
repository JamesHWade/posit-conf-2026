## Abstract

R users have deep domain knowledge and finely-tuned workflows. You shouldn't have to abandon them to use AI. The R ecosystem already has incredible building blocks—ellmer, shinychat, ragnar, and more. deputy and dsprrr complete the stack. Built on ellmer, deputy adds agentic workflows—agents that plan, use tools, and solve multi-step tasks without leaving R. dsprrr lets you program, not prompt, your LLM pipelines—composing modules and letting data-driven optimization do the tuning. With coding agents making it easier than ever to port innovations across languages, R's AI story has never been stronger. Stay in the ecosystem you love—and bring agents with you.

## One-minute video script

Hi, I'm James Wade. For the past three years at posit::conf, I've explored how LLMs fit into the R ecosystem. This year, I want to make a simple case: you shouldn't have to leave R to build with AI.

If you want agentic workflows today, most roads lead to Python or maybe JavaScript. But you chose R for a reason. You've built up domain knowledge, custom workflows, years of intuition. Switching languages means losing all of that context.

The good news? R's foundation is already here—ellmer, shinychat, ragnar. In this talk, I'll introduce some new entrants into the AI ecosystem in R that cover agentic orchestration and programmatic prompt optimization, built on top of the tools you probably already use.

And thanks to coding agents, bringing the best ideas from other languages into R is easier than it's ever been.

Stay in R. Bring agents with you.
